20,000 years ago, you could walk from Britain to Europe. No boat. No bridge. Just land. Archaeologists call it Doggerland. In the Ice Age, the sea was about 120 metres lower. Where the North Sea is today, there were plains, rivers and marshes. People lived here. Stone Age hunter-gatherers fished and hunted across it. Then the ice melted, and the sea crept back in. By around 6500 BC, Britain was an island. About 8,200 years ago, an undersea landslide off Norway sent a tsunami into the North Sea. In time, even the last islands slipped beneath the waves. In 1931, a trawler dragged up a lump of peat from the seabed. Inside was a barbed antler point, left by people who lived there. A lost world, still beneath the North Sea. Follow ORDELVA for more.
